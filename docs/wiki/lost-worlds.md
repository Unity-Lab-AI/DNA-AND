## 🦴 Extinct life

### Mass extinctions

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **End-Ordovician extinction** | About 85% of species, all in the seas (land wasn't settled yet) |
| **Late Devonian extinction** | Reefs and many fish, over a long stretch |
| **End-Permian extinction ("the Great Dying")** | About 90% of species, including the last trilobites |
| **End-Triassic extinction** | About 75% of species; driven by global warming; cleared the way for dinosaurs |
| **End-Cretaceous extinction** | All non-bird dinosaurs, ammonites, plesiosaurs and mosasaurs; mammals took over after |
| **Human-era extinction** | A sixth mass extinction may be under way, driven by one thinking species |

### The first strange animals

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Dickinsonia** | A flat, frilly, rug-like early animal over a meter wide (Ediacaran life) |
| **Ediacaran life** | Soft quilted creatures from before the Cambrian, unlike anything alive now |
| **Anomalocaris** | A fast Cambrian sea hunter with grasping front arms and sharp eyes |
| **Trilobites** | Armored sea arthropods that lived for nearly 300 million years, ended by the Great Dying |
| **Sea scorpions (eurypterids)** | Giant sea and river hunters; Jaekelopterus grew over 2.5 m long |
| **Ammonites** | Coiled-shell relatives of squid, everywhere in the seas until the dinosaurs' end |

### Early fish and the walk onto land

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Placoderms** | Armored fish with bony plates for jaws. Example: Dunkleosteus, an armored hunter up to about 10 m long |
| **Tiktaalik** | A fish with a neck and leg-like fins, halfway onto land |
| **Giant insects** | Oxygen-rich Carboniferous air let insects grow huge. Example: Meganeura, a dragonfly-like hunter with wings about 70 cm across |
| **Arthropleura** | A millipede relative up to about 3 m long, a plant eater |
| **Dimetrodon** | A sail-backed hunter, closer to mammals than to dinosaurs |

### Dinosaurs

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Theropods** | Two-legged meat-eaters, and the line that became birds. Example: Tyrannosaurus, Velociraptor, Spinosaurus, Allosaurus |
| **Sauropods** | Huge long-necked plant-eaters, the largest land animals ever. Example: Brachiosaurus, Diplodocus, Argentinosaurus |
| **Ornithopods** | Beaked plant-eaters, many duck-billed. Example: Iguanodon, Parasaurolophus |
| **Horned dinosaurs (ceratopsians)** | Example: Triceratops |
| **Plated dinosaurs (stegosaurs)** | Example: Stegosaurus |
| **Armored dinosaurs (ankylosaurs)** | Example: Ankylosaurus, with a club tail |
| **Dome-headed dinosaurs** | Example: Pachycephalosaurus |
| **Feathered dinosaurs** | Many dinosaurs had feathers long before flight. Example: Archaeopteryx, between dinosaur and bird |
| **Birds** | The one dinosaur line that survived; every bird alive is a dinosaur |

### Other Mesozoic giants

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Pterosaurs** | Flying reptiles, not dinosaurs; some as big as small planes. Example: Quetzalcoatlus, Pteranodon |
| **Ichthyosaurs** | Dolphin-shaped sea reptiles |
| **Plesiosaurs** | Long-necked, four-flippered sea reptiles |
| **Mosasaurs** | Giant sea lizards, top hunters of the last dinosaur seas |

### Extinct plants

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Scale trees (Lepidodendron)** | Giant club-moss trees of the coal swamps; much of the world's coal is them |
| **Archaeopteris** | One of the first true trees |
| **Seed ferns** | Fern-like plants that made seeds |
| **Glossopteris** | A seed plant once across the southern supercontinent, ended by the Great Dying |

**How it works in the game:**

- On Earth-like worlds, evolution can produce dinosaur-like, trilobite-like or mammoth-like life again, in new forms
- Once a line dies out it never returns as the same line; it stays greyed out on the Tree of Life
- Mass extinctions follow real causes: impacts, supervolcanoes, warming, cooling, oxygen loss, and thinking species
- After a mass extinction, survivors spread fast into the emptied world and new groups rise
- Extinct life leaves fossils; thinking species can dig them up and learn from them
