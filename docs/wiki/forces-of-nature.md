## 🧲 The four forces

| | Force | What it does in your world |
|:-:|---|---|
| 🌍 | **Gravity** | Holds planets, stars and galaxies together. Makes orbits, tides and black holes. Decides whether a world keeps its air |
| ⚡ | **Electromagnetism** | Light, electricity, magnetism and lightning. Every chemical bond, so every molecule and every living thing |
| 💥 | **Strong nuclear force** | Holds atoms' cores together. Powers stars through fusion, and reactors and bombs through fission |
| ☢️ | **Weak nuclear force** | Radioactive decay. Helps stars burn |

## 🌋 Inside a planet

```
   🧑 Crust ── the thin skin we live on
   🪨 Mantle ── hot rock that flows, slowly, carrying heat up
   🔥 Outer core ── liquid metal; churning + spinning = a magnetic field
   ⚙️ Inner core ── solid metal at the center
```

| | |
|--|--|
| 🔄 **Mantle convection** | Hot rock rises and cool rock sinks, moving heat out |
| 🧩 **Plate tectonics** | The crust breaks into moving plates that build mountains, trenches, volcanoes and quakes, and help keep the climate steady |
| 🛡️ **Stagnant lid** | Some worlds have one solid shell instead of plates, and heat escapes slowly |
| 🌋 **Magma and volcanoes** | Melted rock reaches the surface, releasing gases into the air |
| 🧲 **Magnetic field** | Made by the churning core. It shields life from harsh particles, makes auroras, and can switch off and back on over billions of years |

## 📚 Everything in the physics, in full

### The four forces of nature

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Gravity** | Holds planets, stars and galaxies together; makes orbits, tides and black holes; decides whether a world keeps its air |
| **Electromagnetism** | Light, electricity, magnetism, lightning, and every chemical bond that holds molecules and life together |
| **Strong nuclear force** | Glues quarks into protons and neutrons, and holds the nucleus together; powers fusion in stars and fission in reactors and bombs |
| **Weak nuclear force** | Radioactive decay, and turns one kind of particle into another; helps stars burn |

### Particles and atoms

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Up quark** | One of the two quarks that build protons and neutrons |
| **Down quark** | The other quark in protons and neutrons |
| **Proton** | Two up quarks and one down; positive charge; the number of protons decides which element an atom is |
| **Neutron** | One up quark and two down; no charge; changes the atom's isotope |
| **Electron** | Negative charge, circling the nucleus; shared or swapped electrons make chemical bonds |
| **Neutrino** | A nearly massless particle made by nuclear reactions, passing through almost everything |
| **Photon** | A particle of light, the carrier of electromagnetism |
| **Gluon** | The carrier of the strong force |
| **W and Z particles** | The carriers of the weak force |
| **Graviton** *(predicted by science)* | The proposed carrier of gravity, never observed |
| **Nucleus** | Protons and neutrons packed at the atom's center, held by the strong force |
| **Electron shells** | The layers electrons sit in; the outer shell decides how an element reacts |
| **Isotope** | The same element with a different number of neutrons |
| **Radioactive decay** | An unstable nucleus breaks down over time, through the weak force or otherwise, giving off radiation |
| **Fusion** | Light nuclei join into heavier ones and release energy; it powers stars |
| **Fission** | Heavy nuclei split and release energy; it powers reactors and atomic bombs |
| **Chemical bond** | Atoms joined by sharing or swapping electrons; the root of all chemistry and life |

### Inside a planet

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Inner core** | Solid metal at the very center (on Earth, iron and nickel) |
| **Outer core** | Liquid metal around the inner core; when it churns and the planet spins, it makes the magnetic field |
| **Mantle** | Hot rock that flows very slowly, carrying heat up from the core |
| **Crust** | The thin rocky skin we live on |
| **Lithosphere** | The stiff outer shell of crust and upper mantle, broken into plates on Earth |
| **Magma** | Melted rock under the surface; it becomes lava when it erupts |
| **Magma ocean** | A whole planet surface of molten rock, common in young and very hot worlds |

### What moves inside a planet

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Mantle convection** | Hot rock rises and cool rock sinks in slow loops, moving heat out of the planet |
| **Plate tectonics** | The crust broken into moving plates that collide, split and slide, building mountains, trenches, volcanoes and quakes; Earth is the only known planet with it |
| **Stagnant lid** | One unbroken rigid shell with no moving plates; heat escapes slowly through it (Mars, Venus) |
| **Volcanism** | Magma reaching the surface through volcanoes, rifts and hot spots, releasing gases into the air |
| **Geodynamo** | A churning liquid-metal core plus a spinning planet make a magnetic field; it can stop and restart over billions of years |
| **Magnetic field** | The planet's field, from its dynamo; it can flip north and south over time |
| **Magnetosphere** | The bubble a magnetic field makes around a planet, turning away fast particles that are harmful to life |
| **Aurora** | Glowing lights near the poles where particles from the star hit the air |

### Atmospheres

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Troposphere** | The lowest layer, holding most of the air and all the weather; it gets colder as you go up |
| **Stratosphere** | Above the weather; on Earth it holds the ozone layer |
| **Mesosphere** | The cold layer where meteors burn up |
| **Thermosphere** | Thin and very hot; auroras glow here |
| **Exosphere** | The outer edge, where gas thins out into space |
| **Air pressure** | The weight of the air above; it falls off fast with height |
| **Greenhouse effect** | Gases like carbon dioxide and water vapor trap heat; Earth averages about 15 °C instead of about −18 °C |
| **Runaway greenhouse** | Heat traps more heat until the planet bakes; Venus, about 96% carbon dioxide, is around 460 °C at the surface |
| **Thermal escape** | Hot, light gas moves fast enough to leave the planet for good |
| **Hydrodynamic escape** | Strong starlight heats the upper air so much that whole streams of gas blow away, mostly hydrogen |
| **Non-thermal escape** | Air lost through chemistry, particle hits and charge swaps instead of heat |
| **The air of Mars** | Very thin |
| **The air of gas giants** | Deep, with no solid ground below |
