## 🌍 Planets

🌬️ **Every world has its own air**, made of real gases in real shares. A temperate world has about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, like Earth. A desert world has thin carbon dioxide, like Mars. A snowball world has cold nitrogen and methane, like Titan. A gas giant is mostly hydrogen and helium.

### By size

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Sub-Earth** | Much smaller and lighter than Earth. Example: Mercury, Mars |
| **Earth-sized rocky planet** | Rock and metal, about Earth's size. Example: Earth, Venus |
| **Super-Earth** | Rocky, bigger than Earth but well below Neptune; the most common kind found so far |
| **Mega-Earth** | A rocky planet far heavier than a super-Earth |
| **Mini-Neptune (gas dwarf)** | A small planet wrapped in a thick hydrogen-helium air; where super-Earths end and mini-Neptunes begin is unknown |

### By makeup

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Rocky planet** | A stone mantle over a metal core |
| **Iron planet** | Mostly metal core with little rock |
| **Carbon planet** *(predicted by science)* | Rich in carbon instead of oxygen; diamond and graphite instead of ordinary rock |
| **Coreless planet** *(predicted by science)* | Rock all the way through, with no metal core |
| **Helium planet** *(predicted by science)* | A world whose air has lost its hydrogen, leaving helium |
| **Ocean planet** | Liquid-water oceans hundreds of kilometers deep, with no dry land |
| **Hycean world** *(predicted by science)* | A mini-Neptune whose mass is mostly a warm water ocean under a hydrogen-helium air |
| **Desert planet** | Dry rock and sand, little or no surface water |
| **Snowball planet** | A world whose oceans have frozen over, far from its star |
| **Ice world** | Ice from pole to pole, often with water below |

### By heat and orbit

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Lava planet** | So close to its star that its surface is molten rock. Example: Kepler-10b, CoRoT-7b |
| **Chthonian planet** *(predicted by science)* | The bare core left after a giant planet's gas was stripped away by its star |
| **Greenhouse world** | Thick air trapping heat until the surface bakes. Example: Venus |
| **Temperate planet** | In the habitable zone, where liquid water can last. Example: Earth |
| **Tidally locked planet** | One side always faces its star; one side burns, one side freezes, with a twilight ring between |
| **Eccentric-orbit planet** | A stretched orbit that swings close to its star and far away, making wild seasons |
| **Circumbinary planet** | Orbits two stars at once |
| **Rogue planet** | No star at all, drifting through space, warmed only from inside |

### Earth-family examples

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Mercury** | Smallest planet, closest to our own star, almost no air, has a magnetic field |
| **Venus** | About 96% carbon dioxide air, about 460 °C at the surface, no magnetic field |
| **Earth** | Plate tectonics, a magnetic field, oceans and life |
| **Mars** | Thin carbon dioxide air, polar ice caps, no global magnetic field |

**How it works in the game:**

- Every planet is generated live from the world seed; nothing is pre-built
- Bigger, colder planets keep more air; small hot ones lose it
- Where a planet forms (inside or outside the frost line, in or out of the habitable zone) sets its makeup and temperature
- Every rocky planet gets core, mantle and crust; heat decides tectonics or a stagnant lid, volcanoes, and a magnetic field
- Every planet is rated on the 10 world conditions (temperature, gravity, air pressure, air makeup, radiation, water, day length, surface, magnetic shield, local life)
- Hypothetical planet types can be generated but are rare, and they're marked as such

## 🟠 Gas and ice giants

### Kinds

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Gas giant** | Mostly hydrogen and helium, a few percent heavier elements. Example: Jupiter, Saturn |
| **Ice giant** | Mostly water, ammonia and methane "ices" under a hydrogen-helium air. Example: Uranus, Neptune |
| **Super-Jupiter** | Heavier than Jupiter; above a point, extra mass makes it shrink instead of grow |
| **Super-Neptune** | Between Neptune and Saturn in mass |
| **Hot Jupiter** | A gas giant in a tight, fast orbit close to its star, hot and puffed up |
| **Hot Neptune** | An ice giant close to its star |
| **Cold Jupiter** | A gas giant far from its star, beyond about 1 au |
| **Super-puff** | Only a few Earths of mass, but wider than Neptune; as light as cotton candy |
| **Eccentric Jupiter** | A giant on a stretched orbit, swinging near and far |

### Cloud classes

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Ammonia-cloud giant** | Cold giants far out, with ammonia clouds, like Jupiter |
| **Water-cloud giant** | Warmer, with clouds of water |
| **Cloudless giant** | Too warm for clouds, a clear blue color |
| **Alkali-metal giant** | Hot, with sodium and potassium in the air, dark |
| **Silicate-cloud giant** | Hottest, with clouds of rock and iron |

### Layers

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Cloud tops** | Layers of ammonia and water clouds at the top |
| **Molecular hydrogen layer** | Squeezed hydrogen gas, thickening into liquid with depth |
| **Metallic hydrogen layer** | Hydrogen crushed so hard it conducts electricity like metal; makes up most of a gas giant and drives its huge magnetic field |
| **Ice mantle** | In ice giants, hot dense water, ammonia and methane under pressure |
| **Core** | A likely core of rock and metal at the center |
| **Helium rain** | On Saturn, helium falls as droplets through the metallic hydrogen, heating the planet |

### Features

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Belts and zones** | Dark belts and light zones of cloud circling the planet, driven by fast winds and fast spin |
| **Giant storm** | A storm that can last centuries and be bigger than Earth. Example: Jupiter's Great Red Spot |
| **Rings** | Ice and rock circling the planet in thin bands. Example: Saturn's rings |
| **Moon system** | Giants gather many moons, from tiny rocks to worlds with their own air or hidden oceans |
| **Giant magnetosphere** | A huge magnetic bubble; Jupiter's is among the largest structures in the Solar System |
| **Polar auroras** | Bright auroras at both poles |

### Solar System examples

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Jupiter** | The biggest planet, the Great Red Spot, dozens of moons |
| **Saturn** | The great ring system, helium rain, dozens of moons |
| **Uranus** | Spins on its side |
| **Neptune** | The farthest planet from our own star, with the fastest winds |

**How it works in the game:**

- Every giant is generated live from the world seed; nothing is pre-built
- Giants form beyond the frost line; some wander inward to become hot Jupiters and hot Neptunes
- A giant's temperature picks its cloud class, from ammonia clouds when cold to rock clouds when hottest
- Gas giants get clouds, molecular hydrogen, metallic hydrogen and a core; ice giants get clouds, an ice mantle and a core
- Fast spin makes belts, zones and long-lived giant storms
- Giants collect many moons and often rings; some moons can hold life
- Civilizations skim giants for hydrogen, helium-3 and deuterium fuel

## 🌙 Moons

### By how they formed

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Regular moon** | Formed in the disk around its planet, like a mini solar system; close, round, flat orbits |
| **Irregular moon** | A captured asteroid or icy body on a far, tilted, stretched or backward orbit. Example: Triton, Phobos, Deimos |
| **Transitional moon** | Between regular and irregular. Example: Iapetus |
| **Impact moon** | Formed from the debris of a giant collision with its planet. Example: Earth's Moon |
| **Moonlet** | A tiny moon, often inside a ring |

### By surface and inside

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Rocky airless moon** | Cratered rock, no air |
| **Volcanic moon** | Tides squeeze it until it erupts constantly, maybe over a magma ocean. Example: Io |
| **Ice-shell ocean moon** | A crust of ice over a global ocean of liquid water, kept warm by tides; a possible home for life. Example: Europa, Enceladus |
| **Cryovolcanic moon** | Shoots geysers of water vapor and ice through cracks in its shell. Example: Enceladus |
| **Thick-air moon** | A moon with heavy air, weather, and lakes and rain of liquid hydrocarbons. Example: Titan |
| **Frozen dead moon** | Ice and rock, no activity, ancient cratered surface |
| **Habitable moon** *(predicted by science)* | An Earth-like moon orbiting a giant planet in its star's habitable zone |

### By orbit

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Tidally locked moon** | Always shows the same face to its planet; most big moons are locked |
| **Shepherd moon** | A small moon whose pull herds a planet's ring into a sharp band |
| **Trojan moon** | A small moon sharing a bigger moon's orbit, 60° ahead or behind it. Example: the trojan moons of Tethys and Dione at Saturn |
| **Resonant moons** | Moons whose orbits keep a steady beat with each other, squeezing each other with tides. Example: Io, Europa and Ganymede (1:2:4) |
| **Retrograde moon** | Orbits backward, against its planet's spin; usually captured. Example: Triton |
| **Moon of a moon** *(predicted by science)* | A tiny moon circling a moon; none confirmed |

**How it works in the game:**

- Every moon is generated live from the world seed; nothing is pre-built
- Giants collect many moons, both regular and captured; rocky planets have few or none
- Tides from the planet and from neighboring moons heat moons from inside, powering volcanoes and keeping oceans liquid
- A moon only stays inside its planet's sphere of pull
- Passing asteroids and comets can be captured as irregular moons
- Ocean moons and thick-air moons can hold life; a moon's local conditions use the same 10-condition world matrix as planets

## ☄️ Asteroids, comets and dwarf planets

### Asteroids by makeup

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **C-type asteroid** | Very dark, carbon-rich and primitive, little changed since the system formed; the most common type |
| **S-type asteroid** | Stony and silicate, brighter; more common close to the star |
| **M-type asteroid** | Nickel and iron, probably the broken metal core of a shattered small world. Example: Psyche |
| **E-type asteroid** | Very bright and reflective |
| **P-type asteroid** | Very dark, reddish, comet-like, found far out |
| **Rubble-pile asteroid** | A loose heap of rocks held together only by its own weak gravity |
| **Binary asteroid** | Two asteroids orbiting each other, or an asteroid with its own tiny moon |

### Asteroids by orbit

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Main-belt asteroid** | In the asteroid belt between the rocky planets and the giants |
| **Near-planet asteroid** | An orbit that crosses close to a planet's; the ones that can hit |
| **Trojan asteroid** | Sharing a planet's orbit, 60° ahead or behind it |
| **Centaur** | Half asteroid, half comet, orbiting between the giant planets |

### Comets

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Short-period comet** | Returns every few years to a few hundred years |
| **Long-period comet** | Falls in once in thousands of years or more, sometimes only once |
| **Star-grazing comet** | Swoops so close to its star that it often breaks up or burns away |
| **Interstellar comet** | A comet from another star passing through once |
| **Nucleus** | A dark ball of ice and dust a few kilometers across |
| **Coma** | A cloud of gas and dust that boils off as the comet nears its star, up to about 100,000 km wide |
| **Hydrogen cloud** | A huge thin cloud of hydrogen around the comet |
| **Ion tail** | A straight, thin, glowing tail of gas pushed directly away from the star by the star's wind |
| **Dust tail** | A wider, curved tail of dust pushed out by starlight, bending along the comet's path |

### Dwarf planets and far bodies

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Ceres** | The first asteroid ever found (1801), now a dwarf planet |
| **Pluto** | A plutino, locked in a 3:2 beat with Neptune |
| **Eris** |  |
| **Haumea** | Spins so fast it's stretched into an egg shape |
| **Makemake** |  |
| **Classical Kuiper-belt object** | Icy body on a calm, round orbit in the Kuiper belt |
| **Plutino** | Icy body locked in a 3:2 orbital beat with the outermost giant, like Pluto |
| **Scattered-disk object** | Icy body thrown onto a long, tilted orbit by a giant planet |
| **Detached object** | Icy body on a huge lonely orbit beyond everything else. Example: Sedna |
| **Oort-cloud comet** | One of a vast number of icy bodies in the outer shell, tens of thousands of au out |

### Meteors and meteorites

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Meteoroid** | A small rock or grain drifting in space |
| **Meteor** | The streak of light when a meteoroid burns up in a planet's air |
| **Fireball** | A very bright meteor, sometimes exploding in the sky |
| **Meteor shower** | Many meteors at once when a planet crosses a comet's dust trail |
| **Meteorite** | A rock that survives the fall and reaches the ground |
| **Carbonaceous chondrite** | Dark, carbon-rich and full of the chemistry of life; from C-type asteroids |
| **Ordinary chondrite** | The most common stony meteorite, with tiny melted beads inside |
| **Stony-iron meteorite** | A mix of rock and metal |
| **Iron meteorite** | Nickel-iron, from the core of a shattered small world |

**How it works in the game:**

- Every asteroid, comet and dwarf planet is generated live from the world seed; nothing is pre-built
- Stony asteroids are more common close to the star, dark carbon-rich ones farther out, icy bodies beyond the frost line
- Short-period comets come from the Kuiper belt and scattered disk; long-period comets fall in from the Oort cloud
- Comets grow a coma and two tails only near their star; the ion tail always points away from the star
- Near-planet asteroids and falling comets can hit worlds; impact size and effect follow the 10 impact sizes on the space page
- A meteorite's type follows the asteroid it came from; carbon-rich ones can deliver the chemistry of life
- Metal asteroids are mined for metals, icy bodies and comets for water and fuel, carbon-rich asteroids for life chemistry
